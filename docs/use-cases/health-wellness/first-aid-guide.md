# First Aid Guide

## Description
Receive clear, step-by-step first aid instructions for common injuries and emergencies. Includes immediate action steps, what to do while waiting for medical help, what NOT to do (common harmful myths), and when to escalate to emergency services.

## Why Hermes
First aid instructions must be clear, correctly sequenced, and include appropriate safety caveats. Hermes produces structured, actionable guidance and explicitly identifies outdated myths (e.g., "tilt head back for nosebleed") that could cause harm, while consistently directing users to emergency services when appropriate.

## Sample Input
```
Situation: Child has a burn from touching a hot pan — about 3cm wide on the palm
```

## Output Format
```
FIRST AID: Burn Injury (Child, Palm)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES IF:
- Burn is larger than the child's palm
- Burn appears white, brown, or black (deep burn)
- Child is showing signs of shock (pale, cold, confused)
- Burn is on the face, hands, feet, genitals, or over a joint
A 3cm palm burn on a child warrants medical evaluation even if it appears minor.

IMMEDIATE STEPS (Do these now)

STEP 1 — Cool the burn (most important step)
Run cool (not cold) water over the burn for 20 FULL MINUTES.
Start the timer. Do not stop early — 20 minutes has been shown to significantly reduce burn depth.

STEP 2 — Remove jewelry/clothing near the burn
Gently remove any rings, bracelets, or watches before swelling begins.
Do NOT remove clothing that is stuck to the burn.

STEP 3 — Cover loosely
After cooling, cover with a clean, non-fluffy material — cling film (plastic wrap) is ideal. Lay it flat over the burn, do not wrap tightly.
Alternatively: a clean plastic bag for hand burns.

STEP 4 — Pain management
Children's paracetamol (acetaminophen) or ibuprofen at appropriate weight-based dosing for pain.

STEP 5 — Seek medical attention
For a child, all but the tiniest burns should be evaluated. Take to urgent care or A&E.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do NOT apply butter, oil, toothpaste, or cream — these trap heat and increase infection risk
- Do NOT use ice or ice water — causes further tissue damage through cold injury
- Do NOT burst any blisters — the blister is a protective barrier
- Do NOT use fluffy cotton wool directly on the burn — fibres stick to wounds

IMPORTANT DISCLAIMER
This guide provides general first aid information. It is not a substitute for professional medical training or treatment. In any life-threatening emergency, call your local emergency number immediately (911 / 999 / 112).
```

## Tips
- Use for refreshing knowledge before travel, camping trips, or events with children.
- Always call emergency services first in any serious emergency — use this guide while waiting for help.
- Consider taking an in-person first aid course (Red Cross, St John Ambulance) for hands-on training.
- This guide covers common situations; for complex scenarios always defer to emergency services.
